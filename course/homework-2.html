    Instructions: Complete these problems.
    🔗

    1. Other Means.

    1. The Huntington-Hill method uses the geometric mean,
      \begin{equation*} c(L,U) = \sqrt{LU}, \end{equation*}
      which generalizes for more than two numbers as
      \begin{equation*} c(a_1,a_2,\cdots,a_n) = \sqrt[n]{a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n}. \end{equation*}
      What if we modify Huntington Hill to use
      \begin{equation*} c(L_s,Q_s,U_s) = \sqrt[3]{L_sQ_sU_s} \end{equation*}
      where \(Q_s\) is the quota for State \(s\) from the standard divisor? Try on the coffee data set.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. There are two other means we could try, too. The Harmonic Mean is given by
      \begin{equation*} h(L_s,U_s)= \dfrac{2 L_s U_s}{L_s + U_s}, \end{equation*}
      and the Quadratic Mean is given by
      \begin{equation*} p(L_s,U_s) = \sqrt{\dfrac{L_s^2+U_s^2}{2}}. \end{equation*}
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Apply these to the coffee data.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Describe how to generalize each of these to more than two numbers.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    2. Other Methods.

    1. The Adams method rounds up if the quota is above a certain threshold, and rounds down otherwise. What threshold does it use? Try it on the coffee data.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. The Dean method uses the harmonic mean as its threshold. Try it on the coffee data.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    3. Divisor Methods are Equivalent to Rounding Methods.

    Show that for any divisor method, there is an equivalent rounding method that gives the same apportionment, and vice versa.
    🔗

    4. Quota Methods are Equivalent to Rounding Methods.

    Show that for any quota method, there is an equivalent rounding method that gives the same apportionment, and vice versa.

    7. 2024 Electoral College..

    1. Apportioning seats in the House of Representatives also determines each states number of electors in the electoral college. Use the Harmonic Mean to reapportion the House based on the 2020 census. Find a break down of the 2024 election results by state, and use the new Electoral College numbers to see if the outcome of the election would have changed.
