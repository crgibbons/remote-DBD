    Worksheet Hormone Half-Lives and Exponential Decay
    

    In this worksheet, we use differential equations to model how medication levels change over time. Our goal is not to make medical recommendations or predict any individual person’s experience. Instead, we build simplified mathematical models that help us understand how medication levels evolve after treatment changes.
    🔗
    Citations:
    1. Figueiredo, M. G., Gagliano-Jucá, T., \amp Basaria, S. (2021). Testosterone Therapy With Subcutaneous Injections: A Safe, Practical, and Reasonable Option. The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism, 107(3), 614. https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgab772
      🔗
    🔗
    Gender-affirming hormone therapy provides one example where these questions arise in a social and policy context. Clinical guidelines recommend monitoring hormone levels during treatment because concentrations change gradually over time rather than instantly.
    🔗

    1.

    Suppose a medication has concentration \(H(t)\) in the bloodstream at time \(t\text{.}\) A simple model assumes the body removes the medication at a rate proportional to the amount currently present:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dH}{dt} = -kH. \end{equation*}
    Here:
    🔗
    • \(H(t)\) is the hormone concentration at time \(t\text{,}\)
      🔗
    • \(k > 0\) is the elimination rate constant,
      🔗
    • \(t\) is measured in days.
      🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Solve the differential equation
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dH}{dt} = -kH \end{equation*}
    with initial condition \(H(0) = H_0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    The half-life of a medication is the time required for its concentration to decrease to half its initial value.
    🔗
    If the half-life is \(T_{1/2}\text{,}\) show that the constant \(k\) satisfies
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} k = \frac{\ln 2}{T_{1/2}}. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    4.

    A commonly prescribed injectable testosterone formulation has an approximate half-life of \(8\) days.
    🔗
    Using \(T_{1/2} = 8\text{,}\) compute the value of \(k\text{.}\) Then write the concentration function in the form
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} H(t) = H_0 e^{-kt}. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    5.

    Suppose a patient has concentration \(H_0\) at the moment treatment is stopped.
    🔗
    How much remains after:
    🔗
    1. \(8\) days?
      🔗
    2. \(16\) days?
      🔗
    3. \(24\) days?
      🔗
    4. \(32\) days?
      🔗
    Express each answer as a percentage of \(H_0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    A policy that abruptly interrupts access to medication may be described informally as “just stopping treatment.”
    🔗
    Based on the model above, explain why this phrase is mathematically incomplete. Consider the role of half-life, dosage schedule, and the difference between stopping a prescription and the body immediately returning to a previous physiological state.
