    1.

    The model in Part 1 describes what happens after treatment stops. But before treatment stops, medication is usually taken on a schedule. A simple way to model repeated dosing is to update the concentration at regular time intervals.
    🔗
    Suppose a dose is taken every \(T\) days. Between doses, the medication concentration decays exponentially. If the elimination constant is \(k\text{,}\) then after \(T\) days, the concentration is multiplied by
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} a = e^{-kT}. \end{equation*}
    Then a new dose increases the concentration by an amount \(D\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Let \(H_n\) be the medication concentration immediately after the \(n\)th dose.
    🔗
    Explain why the concentration immediately after the next dose satisfies
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} H_{n+1} = aH_n + D. \end{equation*}
    In this model, what does each term represent?
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Suppose the half-life is \(8\) days and a dose is taken every \(7\) days.
    🔗
    First compute
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} k = \frac{\ln 2}{8} \end{equation*}
    and then compute
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} a = e^{-7k}. \end{equation*}
    Interpret the number \(a\) in words.
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    For simplicity, suppose \(D=1\) and \(H_0=1\text{.}\) Use the recurrence
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} H_{n+1}=aH_n+1 \end{equation*}
    to compute \(H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4\text{.}\) Round your answers to three decimal places.
    🔗
    \(n\) \(H_n\)
    \(0\) \(1\)
    \(1\)
    \(2\)
    \(3\)
    \(4\)
    🔗

    5.

    A steady state for the recurrence
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} H_{n+1}=aH_n+D \end{equation*}
    is a value \(H^\ast\) such that if \(H_n=H^\ast\text{,}\) then \(H_{n+1}=H^\ast\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Find a formula for \(H^\ast\) in terms of \(a\) and \(D\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    The recurrence
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} H_{n+1}=aH_n+D \end{equation*}
    is not a linear transformation because of the added constant \(D\text{.}\) However, it can be written as a matrix update by adding an extra coordinate:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{pmatrix} H_{n+1}\\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a & D\\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} H_n\\ 1 \end{pmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Verify that this matrix equation gives the same recurrence.
    🔗
    🔗

    7.

    In this model, regular dosing does not simply keep the concentration constant. Instead, the concentration moves toward a steady pattern determined by the dose size, dosing interval, and half-life.
    🔗
    Explain why this matters when thinking about interruptions to medication access. What information would be missing if we only knew whether someone had taken a dose recently?
